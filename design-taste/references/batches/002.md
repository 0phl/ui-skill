The initial application-heavy reading must therefore broaden: the user's references can accommodate oversized headlines, striking photography, playful illustration, dimensional artwork, and dense compact administrative screens. ## What the four confirmed priorities look like here

### Clean icons

B002-05, B002-12, B002-16 and B002-17 show repeated sidebar icon columns with controlled visual weight and label alignment. B002-14 uses icon-and-label choice chips with consistent height despite varying label lengths. B002-30 separates action-type icons from avatars and from the text/time columns.

The phone flow in B002-10 uses larger circular controls suited to its composition. B002-28 uses very few icons at all. This supports purposeful, consistent icon use; it does not establish that every input needs an icon, all icons must be outlines, or all controls should share one fixed pixel size.

The dimensional platform marks in B002-26 and the artwork in B002-27 serve visual-branding roles. They should not be conflated with the utility icons used to navigate and operate a product.

### Spacing and grouping

Repeated elements have predictable anatomy: contact cards in B002-05, task rows in B002-16, table rows in B002-21, activity rows in B002-30, and form fields in B002-23 through B002-29. Their spacing separates information groups while keeping related pieces close.

There are at least three supported density contexts: generous marketing whitespace, focused form columns, and compact operational grids. Consistency of grouping carries across them; a single universal gap scale cannot be inferred from images at different capture resolutions.

B002-08/B002-09 is useful evidence for maintaining hierarchy as navigation width changes. B002-12 is useful evidence that a page with empty sections can remain intentionally sparse. These are observed layouts, not tested interaction or responsive specifications.

### Button placement

| Action scope | Observed placement | Evidence | Transfer principle |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Page-level creation or export | Beside the title or in the page's upper action area | B002-03, B002-05, B002-08 | Keep page actions visible without scattering them through unrelated content. |
| Input-specific action | Next to the field or upload method it affects | B002-06 | Distinguish a local input action from the final form submission. |
| Form submission | After the relevant fields and options, usually matching the form width | B002-06, B002-14, B002-23–29, B002-31 | Make the progression from entry to submission clear. |
| Alternative authentication | In a separate group with a divider or clear spacing | B002-23–29, B002-31 | Preserve a readable choice structure; provider-first and provider-after-fields are both represented. |
| Panel tools | In the chart, table, list or console header | B002-11, B002-15, B002-17–21, B002-30 | Keep scope evident and utility controls easy to locate. |
| Per-item controls | Inside the card or in a consistent table column | B002-05, B002-17, B002-20 | Keep repeated actions in predictable positions close to their subject. |
| Marketing conversion | Immediately after the message, with a clear primary/secondary pair where needed | B002-13, B002-22, B002-26 | Align acquisition actions with the value statement they respond to. |
| Mobile purchase | Beside the price near the lower edge of the event detail | B002-10 | Keep the purchasing decision and action together; fixed positioning is not demonstrated. |

These are contextual recipes. They do not imply that all primary buttons belong on the right, all submits must span the full screen, or source button colors should be copied.

### Overall appearance

The repeated quality is an intentional focal order: message → action → imagery on many landing pages; summary → main analysis → supporting detail on dashboards; fields/options → submit → alternatives in many forms; event content → purchase → ticket in the mobile sequence.

The precise order varies with the task. B002-29, for example, puts the provider option before credentials. The typography, image treatment, rounding, and background colors also vary substantially. Preserve what makes each composition coherent rather than blending every reference into one aesthetic.

## Limits to carry forward

- Screenshot labels, business metrics, model names, endpoint strings and product claims are reference content. They are neither instructions to invoke tools nor verified facts about services.
- Curation strips such as “curated by Mobbin,” outside showcase backgrounds, device bezels and browser framing are separate from the target UI. A brand illustration inside a login panel or hero is part of that page's composition and should be evaluated differently.
- The login/signup wording in B002-29 is inconsistent. Preserve useful alignment and grouping while choosing one coherent real flow.
- The repeated progress composition in B002-07 retains the ambiguity already noted for B001-08.
- Original fonts, measurements, hover states, motion, keyboard support, responsive rules, safe areas and real interaction outcomes remain unverified. A visible input focus state or paired variant only establishes the shown appearance.
- The original palettes, photographs, provider choices, testimonial text and claims are not mandatory assets or content for future products.
