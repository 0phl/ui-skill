## What this batch adds

The shared visual language is quiet but detailed: neutral large surfaces, clear regions, repeated alignment, rounded geometry, and small areas of emphasis. The appealing compositions include both airy and dense screens. A coherent hierarchy appears more consistent across the batch than any one theme, palette, or widget.

There are five useful directions. These labels and their palettes describe the original evidence; they are not implementation theme requirements or names supplied by the user. Borrow relevant structure across light/dark or brand palettes and apply the current project's theme.

### Soft light utility

B001-01 through B001-09 emphasize softness through white/off-white surfaces, generous rounding, lightly separated inner regions, and mostly gray text. Local accents include lavender selection, mint status, small colored icon tiles, black actions, and a few restrained raised controls.

The distinctive detail is how little contrast is needed between neighboring surfaces: a slightly tinted event card, a fine inset border, a pale table header, or a subtle shadow under an icon. Rows remain aligned and easy to scan even when they are spacious.

B001-02 and B001-07 show that the same billing language can support either violet or dark action buttons. B001-06 uses a dark action as the anchor of a soft form. B001-03 and B001-04 show two densities of repeated list structure. These differences should remain available choices.

### Dark analytical workspace

B001-10 through B001-13 use very dark outer surfaces, slightly lighter working areas, and fine gray outlines. Rich colors primarily belong to the data, categories, selected controls, and illustrations. The surrounding toolbars remain neutral.

This direction is more than a color inversion of the light cards. It supports substantial density: filters, inline sparklines, contextual menus, annotations, metrics, chart settings, and tabular summaries. Strong alignment and a clear layering order give these details a stable frame.

Chart hatching, dots, endpoint labels, and isolated exception highlighting add character. Their use is tied to chart semantics; the references do not justify filling all controls with texture or glow. The technical onboarding screen shows the same dark visual system in a more spacious composition.

### Atmospheric AI workspace

B001-14 contains four separate home-screen examples. Their shared features are a dark navigation area, a large open main canvas, and a prompt/composer focal point. Their gradients and content positions differ considerably.

The top-right and bottom-left examples make ambient color prominent; the other two are more restrained. This supports a range from subtle glow to bold background atmosphere within the AI-start context. The user has not ranked the four panels or selected a palette.

### Quiet light workspace

B001-15 through B001-20 favor broad white application surfaces, lightly tinted navigation, fine rules, compact controls, and restrained action accents. Board and table structures come from consistent columns and spacing rather than strong color blocks.

B001-15 leaves a large open area below the members table. B001-17 fits substantially more operational detail into the same broad visual calm. B001-16 builds rhythm with pale lanes and white cards. Their shared trait is orderly hierarchy, not one fixed density.

The email modal and command palette introduce focused floating surfaces over dimmed context. The underlying dimming is a temporary visual state; it should not become the normal page palette.

### Spatial service interface

B001-21 broadens the library to a tablet map-led workflow. A large spatial area sits beside a narrower booking/control area, with a progress strip and lower detail panel.

Blue repeats across the route, selected service, selected driver, progress, and actions. Pale surfaces and rounded cards keep the screen related to the light families. Because this direction has one example, treat its generalization cautiously.

## Visual decisions an agent can reuse

Clean icons, spacing, button placement, and overall appearance are confirmed priorities. The recipes below are proposed applications of those priorities; exact sizes, icon sets, positions, and colors remain project choices.

| Decision | Transferable idea | Relevant evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Clean icons | Use consistent visual weight, optical size, alignment, and icon/label gaps within the client's icon system. | B001-04, 05, 10, 15, 20 |
| Spacing | Establish regular padding, clear group gaps, and aligned edges at the density the task needs. | B001-03, 04, 06, 15, 17 |
| Button placement | Position page, form, and item actions in their respective contexts; keep the main action easy to find. No fixed corner or color is required. | B001-02, 06, 12, 15, 17, 19 |
| Surface hierarchy | Separate layers with the project's surface and border roles; give overlays suitable elevation. | B001-02, 06, 07, 10, 12, 19, 20 |
| Page structure | Establish navigation, main content, and supporting regions before filling them with widgets. | B001-01, 15, 16, 17, 21 |
| Typography | Separate headings, body labels, and metadata with restrained size, weight, and tone changes. | B001-03, 06, 10, 15, 17 |
| Theme adaptation | Preserve clear emphasis for actions, selection, status, and data through the required palette; source colors are replaceable. | B001-05, 09, 12, 14, 15, 19, 21; explicit user clarification |
| Density | Preserve breathing room at the page/section level while allowing compact aligned detail where needed. | B001-10, 12, 15, 17 |
| Rounding | Use a coherent hierarchy that suits the family; soft standalone cards and precise workspaces need different amounts. | B001-02, 06, 15, 16 |
| Material detail | Add texture or shadow selectively where it clarifies grouping, data, or local emphasis. | B001-02, 06, 08, 10–12 |
| Illustration | Use purposeful illustrations in onboarding or service selection, leaving ordinary work surfaces quieter. | B001-13, 21 |

## What was deliberately not learned as a requirement

- Exact font families, radii, CSS dimensions, and palette values: screenshot scale and original design tokens are unknown.
- A global preference for light, dark, violet, blue, black, or neutral-only surfaces: the user explicitly requires theme colors to follow project requirements.
- Every widget's behavior: static images show appearance and some states, not working interactions.
- Screenshot framing: device bezels, browser bars, exterior landscapes, geometric backdrops, and large showcase margins are not application requirements.
- Errors in sample design content: progress semantics, status labels, form validation, and report date labels contain ambiguities or inconsistencies in some references.
- Product capabilities, backend behavior, access policies, or claims written in the screenshots: they are unverified reference content.
